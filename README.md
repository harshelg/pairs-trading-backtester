# pairs-trading-backtester
A pairs trading strategy backtester which takes two different stocks in Yahoo Finance and analyses their hedge ratio and spread historically, making trades based on whether the spread is wider or narrower than expected and visualising the results of trades made on these signals.

I built a backtested that identifies a cointegrated pair of stocks and constructs a spread using a hedge ratio to trade on mean-reversion signals when the spread deviates in a certain direction. Although the two tickers currently in the project are banking related, they can be substituted and backtested for ANY two pairs of stocks found on Yahoo Finance. Importantly, the best results will come when the two stocks actually have a cointegration (i.e., they are in the same industry or are contingent on similar resources). In this specific example, two banks were used (CommBank and Westpac) as they are both major retail banks in Australia which will have roughly similar market fluctuations.

In my code, I first pulled historical data via yfinance using the range 2015 to 2024 starting relatively late to avoid the knock on effects of the global financial crisis, however including data from COVID due to economic effects that are still relevant in 2026, when this backtester is written. I tested multiple candidate pairs for cointegration including CBA/WBC as well as BHP/FMG, IAG/SUN and XOM/CVX for variety in stock behaviour. I then computed the optimised hedge ratio using OLS regression and built my spread (30 days in code, however this can be tinkered with for different results) and clear cut entry/exit thresholds for z-scores above 2 and below -2.

I then printed a cumulative returns chart as well as the Sharpe ratio and max drawdown, omitting win rate for the moment for simplicity. This allowed me to evaluate the performance of the pair trading strategy numerically rather than via qualitative analysis.

The main limitations for this project included the small (4) number of candidate pairs tested, not providing enough variety in behaviour to determine whether this strategy is always profitable, as well as the lack of transaction cost modelling which is relevant as the trades often occur with small profit and loss values, and the unoptimised parameters such as window and thresholds - these were simply substituted with reasonable starting values and can be improved in future for a stronger trading strategy.
